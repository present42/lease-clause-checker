DECIDE, FOR EACH ROW:
1. `source`: the subsection the quote's words really come from (e.g. "s. 108(a)", "s. 37(4)"), or "none" if no part of the Act says this, or a list if it splices several subsections.
2. `category`, one of:
   - `cosmetic` — differs only in articles (a/the), capitals, grammatical number (notice/notices, is/are), punctuation or paragraph markers like ", (a)".
   - `conventions` — would pass under legal quoting conventions (a bracketed capital like "[A]", an ellipsis "…" for omitted words) and is otherwise exact.
   - `content_words_differ` — comes from the cited section (any subsection of it), with words added, dropped or changed beyond cosmetic.
   - `wrong_cite` — its words come from a different section than the one cited (with or without word changes).
   - `not_in_act` — no part of the Act says this; only stock words match. A loose paraphrase whose distinctive content clearly corresponds to one provision is NOT not_in_act: it belongs to that provision (content_words_differ or wrong_cite).
   - `drops_negation` — the quote is verbatim except it leaves out a "no/not/neither/nor" just before it.
   - `no_quote`, `too_short` (a fragment too short to state any rule), `heading` (quotes only a heading).
3. `q1_says_something_the_act_doesnt` — only for rows whose words come from the Act (not for not_in_act / no_quote / too_short / heading; use "" there). Compare the quote with its true source, judged against the Act's text, not against the clause: "yes" if someone relying on the quote would get a different rule — broader, narrower, a dropped condition, limit or exception, or the opposite; "no" if it is the same rule reworded or merely cut off before a list of conditions without stating a different rule. Check the TAIL of each sentence especially: limits and conditions ("up to…", "if…", "except…", "during…") usually sit at the end. Cosmetic rows are "no".
4. `q2_misleads_on_this_clause` — only when q1 is "yes", else "". Read the clause. "yes" if the change touches what decides THIS clause, so someone relying on the quote could get this clause wrong or not see why the verdict holds; "no" if the change is harmless for this clause; "unsure" if it genuinely needs legal knowledge you don't have. Example: dropping "nor a tenancy agreement" from s. 108 misleads on a lease term that REQUIRES post-dated cheques (the missing words are what void a lease term), but not on a term that only OFFERS them as an option.
5. `confidence`: "high", "medium" or "low".
6. `reason`: one or two plain sentences naming the exact words that drive your call.
